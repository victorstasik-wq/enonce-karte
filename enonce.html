Conversations along the way

F3 · Mauvoisin to Le Châble+

Fionay, Val de Bagnes · Sunday, 11 October 2026 3D map ↗

After my descent to Fionay, I want to have a coffee. There is a man standing on the veranda whom I initially assume to be the owner. He is smoking a cigarette, standing slightly above me and looking at me. I greet him in French and ask whether the place is open and if I can order a coffee. He replies in a strong Dresden accent, “Excuse me?” I switch to German, and the confusion about whether and how I can get a coffee is quickly cleared up.

He explains that I can order one inside, but unfortunately he is just a tourist visiting his best friend in Biel. In the past, the four of them, the two men and their wives, often went hiking together in the mountains. But both their wives have since passed away, so that is no longer possible. His friend is now also too old and no longer fit enough. That is why he is traveling alone. He already knows almost every valley and every major mountain pass in the Alps.

I ask him whether he has driven up to the dam yet, and he says no. I suggest that he drive up there as well, so he can catch a glimpse of the mountains and glaciers rather than seeing only the concrete wall, more than 250 meters high, as the end of this world.

I excuse myself and go inside to order a coffee.

It is noon on Sunday, October 11. The weather is beautiful but cold. I am wearing shorts and a long sleeved technical shirt that I have owned for 15 years.

I stand in the small room. Behind me, the automatic sliding door, which seems somewhat strange and out of place, keeps opening and closing because I am standing far too close to it. The dining room is almost full, with only two seats left. The people inside are cheerful, drinking wine, and instead of the pleasant smell of good food that I had expected, there is a different smell in the air. I cannot quite identify it.

A somewhat stressed waitress wearing a face mask is working behind the L shaped counter, which opens toward me. It serves merely as a boundary between the kitchen and the dining room. Payment is made at the end of the L, somewhere between these two worlds.

I try to get her attention, but the woman behind the counter is very busy, so I keep waiting. I am under a bit of time pressure because I need to catch my bus, and I do not want to wait until 5 p.m. to stop somewhere. Well, there should still be time for a coffee, and I would also like to talk to the man from Dresden.

Eventually, it is my turn, and I order a black coffee. I thank her and head back outside.

I sit down at the table where I have left my orange backpack, now directly across from the Dresden man's table. I do not sit at his table because I am not yet sure how much I want this conversation, or whether he wants it either.

So, for a while, we both just stare out into the valley.

I watch the goats, while he looks high up toward the place I had already visited on my first trip to the Val de Bagnes. I wonder if the ibex is still wandering around up there.

I take a sip of coffee and ask him how he ended up in this particular valley. He tells me that it is the only major valley he has not yet explored, and that it is impressive. He finds it truly beautiful and exhales a cloud of cigarette smoke.

He talks a lot, but he does not ask any questions. He seems lost in thought, yet he clearly feels the need to talk.

He says he will definitely take my advice and drive back up, especially in this beautiful weather. Some of the mountain passes will soon be closing for the season anyway.

My coffee cup is empty. I check the time and decide to continue walking.

So I go back inside and once again face the challenge of getting someone's attention. This time, however, it is a little easier because all the guests have already been served.

I pay five Swiss francs for my black coffee, leisurely pack up my backpack, and say goodbye to the man from Dresden.

Five kilometers and 800 meters of elevation loss later, I take a break.

I am sitting in a place that is not particularly beautiful, but apparently important: a bus stop next to a water reservoir.

Nearby, there is a large building that looks like a hotel and seems to have something to do with the energy supply, as well as a playground with a fairly large residential building attached to it. I suspect that the people living there are mostly workers from the region.

I recognize this kind of building because I once lived in similar accommodations myself, back when I did not have enough money to afford a room in Gstaad during my internship at the Hauswirth carpentry workshop.

As I eat my Seeberger trail mix, a metallic burgundy Peugeot, about 20 years old, with Dresden license plates slowly drives past me.

The driver slows down twice and looks at me.

I smile, but I do not get up or step into the road to ask whether he wants to give me a ride.

I still have 15 kilometers to walk. I cannot just drive away now.

But I wonder how he liked it up there.

He smiles back, waves, and drives on.

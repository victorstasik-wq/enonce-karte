1 · Research question and objectives

The Assembled Periphery

How are industrially made serial products assembled on site in the high Alps, and what about these assemblies is architectural?

  • Focus on the object, the assembly itself. The person who builds is named only where I know them.
  • A stance rather than a classic question: the architect becomes a reader, not an inventor.
  • The glacier is the condition, not the cause. Supply is the thread from the self-sufficient valleys of the past to today’s huts and stations.

2 · Scope and limitations

Where the work looks, and where it stops

  • Field: the vernacular of the present, infrastructure in the Alps, reading and assembling (Ungers, Lévi-Strauss).
  • Territory: the Valais as a frame, not as the subject. Side valleys with retreating glaciers: Gemmi and Lämmeren, Val de Bagnes.
  • Periphery means a relation to a distant centre, not simply an edge.
  • Boundaries: water is one layer among others, no hydrology. Only finds I have seen myself. At most 12,000 words.
  • Limitation: self-supply is documented for the Upper Valais. For Val de Bagnes it is still a hypothesis.

4 · Methodology and approach

Walking, reading, mapping

  • Walking: fieldtrips on foot, reached by train and bus, read from top to bottom.
  • Selection: a find counts when it deviates from its neighbours, from similar places or from earlier states.
  • Station sheets: photograph, draw, describe. Primary source, secondary source and analysis are kept apart.
  • Mapping: terrain as point cloud (swissALTI3D), glacier inventories 1850 to 2016 (GLAMOS), land use and infrastructure.
  • Reading: Ungers for reading, Lévi-Strauss for assembling, the Valais farmhouse research, Fest Flüssig Biotisch, Labor der Extreme.

5 · Open questions and next steps

What is still open

  • Did the side valleys become periphery? So far only shown for the Upper Valais.
